## 2. Frameworks and beat timings

**StoryBrand (SB7), Donald Miller.** Seven slots: a character, has a problem, meets a guide, who gives a plan, and calls them to action, which helps them avoid failure, and ends in success. The customer is the hero, the brand is the guide. Problems have three layers: external (the thing), internal (how it feels), philosophical (why it is wrong that they have it). Sources: https://bigredjelly.com/blog/what-is-storybrand-and-how-do-you-build-a-brand-story-for-your-business/ and https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller

**Before After Bridge.** Before: the reader's present situation, so they recognise themselves. After: the better state, kept believable. Bridge: the product as the route between them, plus a call to action. Sources warn it is easy to overpromise, so keep the "after" attainable. https://www.campaignmonitor.com/email/bab-copywriting and https://pipl.ai/glossary/bab-formula

**Story Spine (popularised by Pixar, created by improv teacher Kenn Adams).** Once upon a time / Every day / But one day / Because of that / Because of that / Until finally / And ever since then. https://www.sessionlab.com/methods/story-spine and https://www.wusf.org/2026-06-23/meet-the-creator-of-the-story-spine-an-8-sentence-tool-to-create-and-analyze-stories

**Hero's journey for short form.** Condensed to "problem, to pressure, to change", with the emotional turn landing fast enough for 15 to 60 s. One playbook says: write the human story with no product in it first, then bring the product in at the shift moment so it is "the bridge, never the hero". https://shortgenius.com/blog/script-writing-techniques and https://adcreate.com/blog/storytelling-video-ads-conversion-frameworks (vendor blogs, moderate weight)

**First 3 seconds, from the platforms.**
- TikTok's own ads help: "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness" and "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds", using "suspense, surprise, or other emotions". It recommends "featuring people such as creators, employees, or customers to capture attention", and text at "5-10 words per second". https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices
- Meta (as reported by Adobe): ads with 9:16, sound on, safe zones and at least one extra element such as "human presence, text overlay, voiceover, or an early hook" saw a 30 percent higher 3 second play rate. https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/11/04/unlock-potential-reels-best-practices-for-creative-success-using-adobe-creative-cloud
- Agency guidance: open with motion, a tight face, or a direct claim about the viewer's situation; a logo card or slow intro wastes the first second. https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/meta-ads-reels-ads-guide (vendor)

**Beat timings for a 30 s ad (vendor sources, not platform rules):** hook 0 to 3 s, relatable problem 4 to 10 s, solution 11 to 25 s, call to action 26 to 30 s. A second version: hook 0 to 5 s, outcome 5 to 10 s, solution 10 to 25 s, CTA 25 to 30 s. https://novoads.ai/blog/video-ad-storyboard and https://gotranscript.com/public/mastering-30-second-video-scripts-8-essential-tips-for-impactful-ads

Scaled to 25 s (my own scaling, not sourced): character and problem 0 to 3 s, the pressure 3 to 8 s, turning point with the playable home 8 to 18 s, the ending moment 18 to 22 s, CTA 22 to 25 s.

## 3. Real buyer pains (Pakistan first)

**Habitt's own rules raise the stakes of a wrong choice.** From https://habitt.com/pages/habitt-policies-procedures :
- "Orders cannot be cancelled once they have been placed."
- Change of mind returns or refunds are not allowed; promotional and discounted products are non refundable.
- Returns only for valid reasons such as damage or a wrong item, reported within 48 hours (24 hours for an exchange, with unpacking video as proof).

So if the sofa colour is wrong for your lounge, it stays. That is a real, sourced stake for the story.

**Delivery cost and wait, from https://habitt.com/pages/delivery-information :** furniture takes "15 to 25 working days". Furniture delivery in Karachi, Lahore and Hyderabad runs from Rs 3,499 (orders up to 50K) to Rs 29,999 (501K and above); other cities pay Rs 15,000 to Rs 49,000.

**What Habitt itself highlights.** Its homepage shows a customer quote: "Bought a bedding set, and the print looks exactly as shown on the website." (https://habitt.com) The fact that "looks like the photo" is the testimonial they chose to show tells you it is a live worry.

**Habitt complaint.** The only public Trustpilot review (1 star, 28 Aug 2026): a bed "broke simply when one person sat on it" within 8 months, "premium price they charge compared with the local market", "I was simply told that the warranty had expired." One review, not representative. https://www.trustpilot.com/review/habitt.com

**Online furniture buying in Pakistan, Zameen guide** (https://www.zameen.com/blog/tips-buying-furniture-online-pakistan.html):
- Items often look different in real life, and buyers "end up hating it".
- Photos vary with lighting, so check customer photos in "different lighting and angles".
- Check measurements and whether the piece fits through your gate and room entrance.
- Delivery charges can appear as a surprise at checkout; returning heavy items costs a lot.

**Couples and families disagreeing.** IKEA's UAE campaign was built on the insight that furniture shopping causes relationship tension, with a couple arguing "game room or study" and another not agreeing on a coffee table in 25 years. https://campaignme.com/my-stuff-vs-our-stuff-al-futtaim-ikea-helps-couples-who-have-disagreements-over-furniture/ . IKEA Place's own launch spokesperson Michael Valdsgaard said many people are uncertain about changing their furniture "because they're afraid of making the wrong decision" and that the app "makes it easier to make buying decisions in your own place". https://www.ikea.com/global/en/newsroom/innovation/ikea-launches-ikea-place-a-new-app-that-allows-people-to-virtually-place-furniture-in-their-home-170912/ and https://www.furnituretoday.com/business-news/ikea-place-conversion-rates-not-our-goal-inspiration

**Why people return furniture (global, mixed evidence).** A vendor benchmark says size and space mismatch drives about 58% of furniture returns and colour or material gaps about 44% (https://eightx.co/blog/average-furniture-and-home-return-rate-benchmarks) **[methodology unverified]**. Consumer Reports' 2020 survey of 38,000+ members found returns uncommon, mostly for damage, with only a small minority over colour or fit (https://www.furnituretoday.com/business-news/consumer-reports-survey-offers-good-news-about-furniture-deliveries). Do not put any of these numbers in the reel.

**Wedding and jahez pressure.**
- The old dowry lists "included a refrigerator, a bedroom set, crockery and gold"; furniture trucks are no longer paraded the same way and openly demanding dowry is now embarrassing in many circles, but the pressure has moved, not gone. Kiva Malick, The News on Sunday, 9 Aug 2026. https://www.thenews.pk/tns/detail/1430919-the-bride-is-the-dowry
- Dawn (older, undated) lists furniture and a bedroom set as core wedding spend and notes the trend "to overspend under social pressure or for show off". https://dawn.com/news/167287
- The Tribune calls jahaiz a cost on top of the wedding itself that pushes families into loans. https://tribune.com.pk/article/16173/jahaiz-destroying-families-one-wedding-at-a-time
- Winter is Pakistan's wedding season. https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/brides-biryani-and-marriage-multiplexes-pakistans-wedding-season-heats-up-in-cool-weather

Caution: jahez is socially charged (Molty's dowry mattress ad drew criticism, see section 1). A story about a couple choosing their own first home together is safer than one about a father buying dowry.

**Not found with sources:** Pakistani forum quotes in buyers' own words on colour mismatch or "can't picture it". Needs a manual Reddit pass.

## 4. "Try before you buy" ads and how they frame the person

- **IKEA Place (2017):** framed around the person in their own place: "easier to make buying decisions in your own place"; scales products to the room with 98% accuracy. Its launch campaign, Matchers Keepers, made the story about two people agreeing, not about AR. Sources above.
- **IKEA Kreativ (June 2022):** scans your room, makes a 3D replica, and deletes your existing furniture so you can try IKEA pieces in its place. Press framed it as "IKEA's app update can delete your furniture and replace it with theirs". https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/ikeas-app-update-removes-furniture-37097182 and https://www.designboom.com/technology/ikea-ai-app-replaces-room-designs-and-furniture-06-23-2022/ . No Kreativ story ad found.
- **Wayfair View in Room 3D (2017, photo mode 2019):** pitched as judging fit "without a measuring tape" and previewing furniture "in their home before placing an order" (co founder Steve Conine). https://techcrunch.com/?p=1609428 , https://www.engadget.com/2019/11/14/wayfair-shopping-app-ar/ , https://digitaltrends.com/home/wayfair-view-in-room . No View in Room story ad found.
- **Planner 5D, Roomstyler, Houzz:** no story ads found in this pass.

Takeaway: the tool launches were sold with feature language, but the one that told a story (Matchers Keepers) put two people and a disagreement first and used the tool as the way they reach agreement.

## 5. Vocabulary: flat, apartment, house, home

- Zameen, Pakistan's biggest property portal, labels Karachi rentals "Flat For rent" and its URLs say "Rentals_Flats_Apartments"; its blogs switch between "flat" and "apartment" for the same thing. https://www.zameen.com/Rentals_Flats_Apartments/Karachi-2-1.html and https://zameen.com/blog/affordable-areas-rent-flats-karachi.html
- Zameen also says Karachiites are used to "community living in an apartment complex", while Lahoris more often live in houses with their own parking and garden. https://www.zameen.com/blog/popular-apartment-types-pakistan.html
- A Pakistani thesis on real estate ads found code mixing (English with Urdu) used to grab attention. https://escholar.umt.edu.pk/signposting/describedby/294b4468-37c0-4ce7-88ee-a08a47870b8a

Reading: "flat" is natural and not confusing in Pakistan, especially Karachi, but it excludes the Lahore and Islamabad house owner. "Ghar" and "home" cover everyone. "Pehla ghar" (first home) appears to be a Habitt banner name **[unverified]**. Since narration is English only (locked 2026-10-09), use "home" or "first home" in voice; "flat" is fine as a caption detail if the character lives in Karachi.

## What this means for a 25 s Playable Spaces reel

Shared rule from the research: the person and their moment in the first 3 seconds, the playable home enters at the turning point as the bridge, the ending is a feeling plus one clear action (play it, tap to buy). No invented statistics. Real Habitt facts that can carry stakes: no change of mind returns, 15 to 25 working days for furniture.

**Concept A: "Can't send it back" (Before After Bridge)**
- Character: a young woman in Karachi furnishing her first flat on her own salary.
- Problem, her words: "If this sofa looks wrong in my lounge, I'm stuck with it." (Grounded in Habitt's no change of mind returns.)
- Turning point: she opens the playable home, drops the sofa in, swaps it from grey to green, moves it by the window, flips the light to evening.
- Ending: she taps the real price, then Buy, and smiles at the room she already knows. Line: "Now I know before I buy."

**Concept B: "Grey or green?" (StoryBrand)**
- Character: a newly married couple setting up their first home together.
- Problem, their words: he wants grey, she wants green, and neither can picture the other's choice. (Grounded in the IKEA UAE couple insight and Matchers Keepers.)
- Guide and plan: the playable home. Each swaps the colour, they walk it through daylight and night.
- Failure avoided: buying a sofa one of them hates and living with it. Success: they agree in one minute, tap Buy together. Line: "Argue in 3D. Agree at home."

**Concept C: "Before Ammi sees it" (Story Spine)**
- Character: a son in Lahore or Islamabad redoing the family lounge before Eid guests or a wedding in the family.
- Every day: the old room. But one day: the family is coming. Because of that: he must get it right first time, no returns, weeks to deliver. Because of that: he builds the room first in the playable home, moving pieces, changing the time to evening when guests arrive. Until finally: he taps through and orders. Ever since: the room looks exactly the way he played it.
- Line: "Play it first. Buy it once."
- Caution: keep it about guests and pride in the home, not dowry, given the Molty backlash.

All three end on the same CTA: the live link to play the home and the real Habitt buy links. Run each line through the no-ai-slop and verify-before-ship gates before recording.
